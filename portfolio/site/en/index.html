02Consumer goods · Experiential brand Japan Tobacco InternationalAndorra · 2011

Camel · Music territory

A tobacco brand cannot buy media. So instead of proposing a campaign, I proposed a territory, and then everything the brand needed to live inside it.

02 / AR DiscovrCamel
Brief
Japan Tobacco International needed presence for Camel and Winston in a market where tobacco advertising is closed by law. No media, no conventional campaign, no product claims.
What I did
Pick a territory, music, and build the whole of it. Brand presence at major festivals, with camel sculptures used as projection-mapping surfaces. Curated music at point of sale. And AR Discovr, an augmented-reality app that turned the pack itself into a marker: you pointed your phone at it and a player appeared to discover and download new music. Built in 2011. As a permanent venue, we took over the Tabola nightclub in La Massana and ran it for three years as the brand’s home.
Outcome
A brand with continuous presence and a territory of its own in a market where advertising was impossible. The augmented-reality app dates from 2011: the iPad was one year old and brand AR was not yet a category.
Role
Territory strategy + Concept + Digital product + Running the operation

04Trade show stand · Corporativo SIL Barcelona2008

Abertis Logística

A logistics network is invisible and, frankly, dull. The job was to make it visible in a trade fair hall, where you have about three seconds to stop someone.

04 / Stand y pantallaAbertis
Brief
Abertis Logística wanted a different kind of stand for SIL, the International Logistics Exhibition. The real problem was not the space, it was the subject. You cannot bring motorways and warehouses into a hall.
What I did
I worked on two levels. Structurally, suspended ring volumes in the corporate orange and navy, links of the chain the company actually sells. Editorially, I gave every node of the network a face: one visual piece per location fusing the identity of the city with photographs of its real platform, so Lisbon arrived with its tram and its terminal, Álava with its dolmen and its warehouses, Santiago de Chile with the Andes. Backlit totems carried them across the floor and a large LED wall carried them at scale.
The technical piece
That wall read a camera feed as a grid and, by measuring colour change cell by cell, calculated the direction and speed of people walking past, launching graphics that moved with them. This was 2008, two years before Kinect brought that kind of sensing to the mainstream, so all of it was custom-built.
Outcome
Presented to Abertis’s management the morning of the opening. The night before we found that large flat areas of the corporate orange rendered unevenly across LED panels; we swapped a module and rebalanced the whole image-generation area until it was clean. On a stand you can absorb almost anything, except the brand looking wrong.
Role
Concept + Art direction + Stand design + Production direction
